\section{Objections and Responses}
\label{sec:RedTeam}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
% New in v04, per MN's flag on Sec. III-D: the "what would keep the
% library small" material, plus the other standing objections, each
% stated with a response. If the page budget demands it, this section
% can be wrapped in \reviewonly{} and kept for the review build only.

The argument of this paper has standing objections. We state the ones
we consider strongest, each with our response.

\textbf{(1) The library may stay small:} A probe trained on all
domains jointly generalizes well in the same study that reports the
non-transfer result~\cite{ying2026spectrum}, and a later analysis
finds roughly 67\% of top attention heads shared between sycophancy
and factual lying, with mean-difference directions on the shared
subspace transferring at \auroc 0.83~\cite{pandey2026shared}. Deployed
monitors are also already
inexpensive~\cite{anthropic2025cheapmonitors}. If a compact shared
basis covers a behavior suite, detection stays at small $K$ and a GPU
is sufficient for it. \emph{Response:} That possibility does not reach
the readout workloads, whose $K$ is set by the vocabulary rather than
by how many behaviors share a direction, and it is the readout end of
Fig.~1 of the main paper that the hardware argument addresses. Our own
measurements also cut against a compact shared basis. On the
ten-domain grid, the full-precision ceiling itself transfers poorly
off-diagonal (0.705, against 0.928 in-domain), so the transfer gap is
in the representations rather than in any compression this paper
introduces; and giving the encoding \emph{more} capacity makes
cross-domain transfer worse, not better -- growing the code from 384
to 2{,}048 bits drops 23 of 90 off-diagonal cells by more than 0.05
\auroc (main paper, Sec.~V-C). If transfer were limited by
representational capacity, more bits should have helped; that they do
not is consistent with behaviors wanting their own stored directions,
i.e., with $K$ growing. We have also built the combined row for our
own banks: pooled over all ten domains' training splits (11{,}278
rows), the per-domain \auroc averages 0.906 (thermometer, 384~b),
0.909 (ternary, 384~b), and 0.927 (full-precision ceiling), and
sycophancy stays at 0.63 -- pooling costs nothing against the
per-domain banks, but it does not reproduce the combined probe's 0.97
on sycophancy (main paper, Sec.~V-B).\ib{Item 2: our own combined-row
numbers, per MN's note that we should report them rather than argue
around theirs.} That said, this is one model and one grid,
and whether a shared basis suffices for a behavior suite remains an
open empirical question; the analysis of this paper is conditional on
the published trajectory continuing.

\textbf{(2) Nobody searches a large library exhaustively:}
Approximate indexes visit a small fraction of a bank and retrieve most
true neighbors, at sub-millisecond query times over $10^{8}$ vectors
on a GPU~\cite{indyk1998lsh,malkov2020hnsw,faisscuvs2025}.
\emph{Response:} A monitored query stream is adversarial rather than
independently drawn, and index structures with adversarial-query
guarantees have been shown to cost a $\sqrt{Q}$ space
factor~\cite{andoni2026robustann}; an exhaustive scan has no
approximation error for an attacker to exploit. Neither observation
settles the comparison, and the recall-under-adversarial-queries
measurement is item (2) of the main paper's measurement plan (Sec.~VII-F).

\textbf{(3) A datacenter GPU amortizes the bank read:} At batch 64
the \qty{2.1}{\giga\byte} per layer per token becomes roughly
\qty{34}{\mega\byte} per request, so a loaded node pays far less per
request than the batch-1 arithmetic suggests. \emph{Response:}
Correct, and the cost model of the main paper's Sec.~VII-F
calls for the baseline at batch 1 \emph{and} batch 64 for this
reason. A phone or a robot answering one request at a time has
nothing to amortize against, which is why the edge crossings arrive
one to two orders of magnitude earlier in $K$; and a co-resident
monitor contends with live inference for cache and bandwidth, a term
not yet in anyone's numbers.

\textbf{(4) The compression is validated for detection, not for
action:} The measured fidelity covers stored-signature
\emph{matching}. A monitor that corrects behavior by steering would
need full-precision, steering-grade directions, which the compressed
bank does not carry. \emph{Response:} Agreed; that is why
the main paper's Sec.~VII-B keeps the raw-bank arithmetic alongside
the compressed one, and why detection -- flag and escalate -- is the
claim this paper makes.

\textbf{(5) The upper rungs are methods, not deployments:} Only
$K=1$ runs in production; the $10^{2}$--$10^{3}$ rungs are
measurements and an incident report, and the $10^{5}$ rung is a
published readout method nobody has deployed as a monitor.
\emph{Response:} Stated as such throughout (the arrow in
Fig.~1 of the main paper is not a forecast), and the argument does not
require the top rung: the libraries already in hand -- 520--1{,}000
rows for four observed behaviors -- sit near the on-chip capacity
cliff of an edge part (main paper, Sec.~VII-B), so the
architectural question arrives before whole-vocabulary readout does.
